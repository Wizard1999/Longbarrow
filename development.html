01 — The miniature myth

A battlefield that feels less like a map and more like a living artifact.

Longbarrow is an RTS about civilizations that inherit a world they do not fully understand. Ancient technology has fossilized into landscape. Knowledge is excavated rather than purchased. Territory changes physically under the forces that control it.

The camera looks down on the conflict like a war table brought to life—broad enough to read an empire, close enough to see moss growing through a soldier’s armor.

02 — Three visions of inheritance

No traditional heroes.
No disposable villains.

Each faction expresses a different relationship with the world that came before.

I

The Cohort

The last command still being obeyed.

Bone-pale constructs, fossilized war machines, and caretakers preserving duties whose creators vanished ages ago. Their weapons are literal light. Their ruins do not look manufactured—they look excavated.

  • Disciplined formations
  • Ancient light weaponry
  • Monumental defensive control

II

The Mycora

Life thriving past the point of mercy.

A hive intelligence that pushes and pulls like the tide. Units are temporary shapes within a larger infection. Death leaves behind moss, fungus, flowers, vision, and more domain for the whole.

  • Tidal swarm behavior
  • Territory born from death
  • Iridescent living mutation

III

The Conclave

A civilization held together by flow.

Water and fabric shaped into bodies, cities, and ritual. The Conclave should feel graceful but never fragile: a culture whose architecture, movement, and power are inseparable from currents.

  • Fluid repositioning
  • Ritual coordination
  • Water-and-cloth material language
